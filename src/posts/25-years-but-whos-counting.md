I remember with clarity the day my parents celebrated their 25th wedding anniversary. It was the day I gave birth to my daughter Lucy; making them grandparents for the second time.  A week later I hosted a dinner party at my place to celebrate this special milestone. I remember being slightly in awe of my parents; their example of faithful commitment and deep love for each other was impactful to me. It was definitely something to be celebrated. I was still in the early years of being married and surmised that for me to get to that milestone I would have definitely grown old with Chris. Writing now from the perspective of my own 25th anniversary, which we celebrated earlier this year, I can’t quite believe I’m at that same stage. Where did that time go? Am I really that old? My memory bank and brain fog remind me in equal measure that a lot of years have passed. Chris and I have had quite the journey together, yet in many ways it seems like only yesterday that we were young, with heads full of dreams and the energy to chase after them.

The year that has marked 25 years of married life has been quite fitting. The first 6 months alone saw us face together, a farewell to a close friend, a shock redundancy, 2 funerals, an overseas wedding, significant health challenges, lots of uncertainty and a child move out of home to list but a few. It’s been quite the year! Nothing about our life together has been boring; every year brings fresh challenges and new delights.

When Chris proposed to me we were both 19 years old. The unique stage of life when you feel very grown up and undeterred by the wisdom of others (the mindset of every school leaver), but in reality you know very little (a problem only solved by life experience.) I said yes, as I loved him and being my best friend I couldn’t imagine a better person to spend my life with. We had been in the same class since year 7 and became close friends throughout the remainder of high school. He was the cute geeky shy boy who clearly loved his science and maths. I was the friendly chatterbox who had big emotions and felt most comfortable on a theatre stage. Proof that opposites do indeed attract.

What followed quickly on the heels of our engagement came the biggest test to our relationship up until that point - our anxieties. Anxiety is something both of us have struggled with in different seasons of our life and often for very different reasons. However, in our engagement, our anxiety was catching. It then became crippling as it threatened to derail us. As a young person I remember having impressed on me that as far as important decisions go, the most important decision I would ever have to make, after my choice to follow Jesus would be who I married, if I married. So when we hit growing pains, as every young couple invariably does, my anxiety grew around this question, “Was Chris the person God wanted me to marry? What if I made a big mistake? Could I know the right person to marry at 20 years old, if I was still growing up myself?“

Chris and I were engaged for 2 and half years, and boy were they tough years. During this time God did a lot of good work in our lives, essentially preparing us for marriage. It was a season of testing. Was our high school romance just for high school, or was it indeed built upon a firmer foundation? Was our relationship centred on Christ; of having shared values that aligned? Were we both willing to relinquish each other if that was best for us? Were we willing to trust God for our future, putting him first in our lives? Trusting God was and still is being willing to follow God’s leading, as I believe that God is good and works for my good. I couldn’t predict what tomorrow would bring, let alone 25 years down the road, but I had put my trust in a good and kind God that does.

As I have reflected upon those early years of our romance, I am so grateful to God for his faithfulness and care towards us. How far we’ve both come! While I am still prone to anxiety, there are some wonderful perks to growing older, and one is that I have a large volume of remembrances of God’s leading, provision and kindnesses to me. My fears and questions may be different to that of my youth, but time and time again God has patiently and faithfully led me to safe harbours. One thing however has never changed for me - my deep love for Chris. It’s still growing! I’m so grateful to God for him and for the life we have made together. It’s been a great adventure thus far; we make a good team! We have raised four young people, who are now the age we were when they were born! We joke with our eldest son that we had two children by his age, which he promptly points out was utterly crazy. Crazy maybe, but no regrets. I look forward to what another 25 years might hold for us.
